For my favorite food, i often eat not as a fancy, luxury types of food. To me, an ordinary culinary is best for me. Especially i'm from an ordinary family, i may eat only a small portion of soup, with fried tempeh, tofu, and a few fish.
- Fried Rice: I do like fried rice because not only it's tasty and delicious, it's also simple with only few i  ngredients. And the most important is all of them are affordable. 
- Stir-fried Kwetiaw: When i was a child, we do visit a restaurant where it has chinese cuisine. I remember when we live in South Tangerang, every Sat night we visit a chinese restaurant and order "Beef Stir-Fried Kwetiaw". It's taste is erally unique, it felts like the flavour is different but it makes me drooling and addict to it. I don't know if it uses lard, but as i remember it's not.
- Capcay (another chinise cuisine): It is a mixed vegetables. Unlike soup, the first method used for is a stir frying. All the ingredients gives all the flavour is stirred, so when the water is poured into the frying pan, it combines and mixed perfectly. And my favorite version is when the broth is thick with a small of corn flavour, that's why it looks convincing. Again, with a chinese ingredients.
- Martabak Telur: 'Telur' version is salty. Many people eat with a spesific sauce that may contains tamarind, salt, sugar, and a few seasoning with adding few pickles. And they eat while it still hot. For me, i often take as a side dish with rice. Not sure why, but i really enjoy. Although mostly it's made from egg, green onions, shallot, garlic, and covered with spring rolled wrapper.
- Martabak Manis: It's a popular dessert, not only for local but also tourist. The texture is squishy, thick, and melting when it enters every mouth. It is also made from dough, with many options combine depend which one you like. There's choco, peanut, cheese, sweet condensed milk, and lots of sugar. It is really sweet, but it doesn't make someone felt sick. It is really delicious, i can only say just taste it first then you'll understand why.
